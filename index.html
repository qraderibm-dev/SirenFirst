01 · The short version

What the project actually does

An ambulance is approaching a junction. The camera cannot see it clearly yet, but the audio can already hear the siren. SirenFirst raises a tentative alert, confirms the vehicle as it gets closer, works out which arm of the junction it is using, and then switches a simulated signal safely.

Layer 1

Hear

Extract the audio track. Detect the siren's sweep pattern even when the ambulance is far away or hidden behind traffic.

Layer 2

See

Track moving vehicles and check for ambulance evidence: flashing red/blue beacon, white or yellow body, red cross, 108 markings.

Layer 3

Locate

Map the road to a bird's-eye view. Which arm is it approaching from? How many seconds until the stop line?

Layer 4

Act

A safety-checked state machine clears conflicting traffic and gives green to the ambulance's arm.

The one rule that keeps it safe Sound can alert. Only sight plus geometry can switch the signal. A siren alone never gets a green light.

02 · The constraint

Why this is not a YOLO project

The professor's rule is “no frontier models like YOLO”, clarified to mean no pretrained weights. Training from scratch is allowed. That single rule changes the project from “fine-tune a detector” into “build and defend a system”.

The usual final-year version

Download. Fine-tune. Draw boxes.

  • Fine-tune YOLO on a small ambulance dataset.
  • Report mAP and a few screenshots.
  • One black box decides everything.
  • Banned by the professor's constraint.
  • No story for “without lag”, lanes, or signal safety.
SirenFirst

Build the evidence, then the decision.

  • Handcrafted and learned cues, trained from zero.
  • Every stage is explainable in the viva.
  • Audio provides early warning; vision and geometry confirm.
  • Arm-level preemption with safety constraints.
  • Benchmark, ablations, and a lead-time vs false-alarm curve.
Honest note YOLO is excellent. This is not an anti-YOLO argument. It is simply excluded here, so the project's value has to come from the system design, the fusion policy, and the evaluation — which is a better final-year story anyway.

04 · The problem

Why Indian junctions need a different design

“Detect an ambulance from a video and let that lane pass automatically, without any lag.” — the given problem statement

Lanes are a suggestion, not a rule

Indian junctions often have no usable lane markings, and ambulances weave through gaps. A design that depends on painted lanes will fail. SirenFirst preempts the approach arm (north, east, south, west) and a free corridor instead.

Sirens travel farther than pixels

A siren is audible from roughly 100–300 m; a camera may only recognise the vehicle at a fraction of that distance. That gap is the entire “without lag” opportunity.

Every second matters

The useful metric is not “did we detect it eventually?” but “how many seconds before the stop line did we confirm it, at an acceptable false-alarm rate?”

GPS geofencing exists, but needs hardware

Bengaluru traffic police geofenced about 80 GPS-equipped ambulances to auto-green 10 junctions (2024). SirenFirst is camera-and-audio only: no fleet instrumentation.

The reframe We are not building “an ambulance detector”. We are building an explainable, multi-cue emergency preemption system with a measurable lead-time advantage. That is what the professor can grade as novel.
05 · The architecture

The idea in one picture

Two independent evidence paths feed one cautious decision. Audio supplies early warning; vision supplies identity; geometry supplies location and time. The fusion gate decides whether the controller is allowed to act.

Audio evidence Visual evidence Geometry Fusion gate Action path

Why the unit is an “arm”, not a lane

In dense Indian traffic, lane markings are often absent or ignored, and an ambulance may approach on the wrong side or through a gap. Asking “which painted lane?” is the wrong question. SirenFirst asks: which arm of the junction is this track entering from, and when will it cross the stop line?

That gives a robust, India-specific formulation: preempt one approach arm, hold the conflicting arms, and reopen the corridor. The bird's-eye view makes the reasoning visible in the demo.

06 · Under the hood

The four layers, explained simply

Each layer has one job, a simple failure mode, and something that can be tested separately. That separation is what makes the project explainable and the evaluation honest.

LAYER 01

Hear — siren detection

early warning

In plain words: listen to the video's audio and decide whether an emergency siren is present, even when the vehicle is too far away to recognise.

How it works

Short audio windows → log-mel, MFCC, spectral flux and F0-sweep features → GMM/SVM classifier (from scratch) → K-of-N smoothing.

Why it matters

Sound arrives earlier than pixels. This is the layer that creates the “without lag” advantage and works when the view is blocked.

What it cannot do

It cannot tell direction with one microphone, and horns, alarms or music can fool it. Hence it only raises a tentative alert.

What we measure

Recall on real 108 clips, false positives per hour on negative traffic audio, and detection latency.

16 kHz mono 1.0 s window 0.25 s hop wail / yelp / hi-lo trained from scratch
Technical details
Features: 64-band log-mel, 20 MFCCs plus deltas, spectral flux, harmonic energy and an F0 track in the 500–2,500 Hz band. Baseline classifiers: a Gaussian mixture per class and an RBF SVM. The optional stretch model is a small CNN on log-mel, trained from zero. Temporal decisions use 5-of-7 window consistency at 0.25 s hop. The operating threshold is chosen from the false-positives-per-hour requirement, not from accuracy alone. Training uses AudioSet-EV v2, the Asif et al. set and sireNNet; cross-dataset tests use UrbanSound8K and ESC-50 siren classes.
LAYER 02

See — visual confirmation

confirmation

In plain words: look at the moving vehicle and check for the things that make it an ambulance, not just any white van.

How it works

Background subtraction proposes moving blobs → Kalman + Hungarian tracking → per-track cues (beacon blink, livery, red cross, 108) → HOG+SVM score.

Why it matters

It prevents the system from switching a signal for a siren-like sound or a white tempo traveller with a roof rack.

What it cannot do

It is weaker than a pretrained detector on tiny or heavily occluded vehicles. That is why it is a gate, not the trigger.

What we measure

Track-level precision and recall, cue ablations, and false tracks per minute.

MOG2 / KNN Kalman + Hungarian beacon FFT red cross · 108 templates no YOLO · no OCR
Technical details
Background subtraction runs on a fixed camera with shadow detection and morphology. Tracks must pass minimum-hit and maximum-age rules. The beacon cue computes red/blue pixel energy in the top 20% of each track box over a rolling buffer and looks for a 1–4 Hz FFT peak; this distinguishes an active beacon from painted red/blue stripes. Livery cues use HSV ratios for white and yellow bodies, plus self-made template matching for a red cross and the digits “108”. The classifier is a HOG + linear SVM trained on Indian ambulance crops and hard negatives; a tiny from-scratch CNN is an optional ablation. No OCR library is used.
LAYER 03

Locate — arm and ETA

India-specific

In plain words: draw the junction from above and ask two questions: which arm is it entering from, and how many seconds until it reaches the stop line?

How it works

One-time 4-point homography → bird's-eye coordinates → approach-arm polygons and stop lines → smoothed velocity → ETA.

Why it matters

It turns detection into a decision: which movement to preempt and when. It also rejects vehicles that are leaving the junction.

What it cannot do

It assumes a fixed camera and a reasonable calibration. Camera shake or a moved tripod invalidates the mapping.

What we measure

Arm accuracy, ETA error at the stop line, and the resulting lead time.

4-point homography N / E / S / W arms stop line EMA velocity lane-less reasoning
Technical details
The homography is computed once per camera from four road-plane points with known spacing. Tracks are mapped to metres, and velocity is smoothed with an exponential moving average. Approach-arm assignment uses the entry zone of the track plus its heading, not a lane index. The ETA is the remaining distance along the arm divided by the smoothed speed. A track is eligible only while it is approaching, inside the junction region of interest, and has enough history. The demo renders the arm and ETA on the bird's-eye inset.
LAYER 04

Act — safety-constrained controller

software only

In plain words: a careful traffic officer inside the computer. It requests priority, clears conflicts, gives green, and returns to normal — but only if the evidence is confirmed.

How it works

Finite-state machine: normal → request → all-red clearance → preempt green → recovery. Abort on track loss.

Why it matters

Safety is explicit. A detection mistake cannot create a conflicting green, and recovery is part of the design.

What it cannot do

It cannot control real hardware in this version, and it cannot guarantee behaviour of human drivers.

What we measure

Zero conflicting greens, clearance respected, response latency, and recovery time.

min green yellow + all-red conflict matrix abort on loss event log
Technical details
States: NORMAL_CYCLE, REQUEST, CLEARANCE, PREEMPT_GREEN, RECOVERY, ABORT. Inputs: confirmed track, approach arm, ETA, time since request, current phase. Constraints: minimum green for the active phase, yellow and all-red clearance intervals, a conflict matrix that forbids simultaneous greens for conflicting arms, a maximum preemption duration, and a safe abort/recovery path if the track disappears. Every transition is written to a JSON event log for the demo and the evaluation.
07 · The contribution

What is actually new here

The professor wants a novel contribution. “I trained a model” is not enough. The defensible contribution is a policy, an India-specific formulation, and a benchmark — each with its own experiment.

01

Asymmetric, two-stage fusion

Audio may alert, but only visual + geometric evidence may act. Prior work fuses scores symmetrically. We report the lead-time vs false-alarm trade-off of the two-stage policy.

02

Arm-level, lane-less preemption

Instead of painted-lane detection, infer the approach arm from bird's-eye trajectories and preempt that arm with explicit safety constraints — designed for Indian junctions.

03

SirenFirst-108 benchmark

A curated South India 108 evaluation set: real cited event clips, disclosed synthetic clips, and negatives, with a reproducible annotation and lead-time protocol.

Fourth, supporting claim Everything is trained from zero and explainable. That is not just a constraint — it enables clean cue ablations (audio only, vision only, fusion; with/without beacon) that a black-box detector cannot provide.

10 · The demo, second by second

One event, from siren to green

An illustrative timeline, not a measurement. It shows the intended behaviour and the exact moment where each layer is allowed to influence the next.

0–3 s · warningThe amber UI says “siren suspected”. Nothing switches. This is where the lead-time advantage is created.
3–4.5 s · evidenceBeacon, livery, 108 and geometry must agree. If they do not, the system stays tentative.

11 · Execution

How we actually achieve it

The plan is deliberately boring: build the smallest testable loop first, prove each layer on saved data, then integrate. No layer is allowed to be a demo-only illusion.

1Freeze the protocol

Define events, arms, lead time, false alarms, and the split before modelling.

2Build baselines

Energy/spectral audio baseline and motion-only vision baseline. They set the floor.

3Train from zero

GMM/SVM audio, HOG/SVM vision, then optional tiny CNNs as ablations.

4Calibrate geometry

One junction, four arms, measured stop lines and bird's-eye mapping.

5Integrate the gate

Audio tentative, visual confirmation, safety FSM, event log.

6Evaluate honestly

Real and synthetic separately, ablations, failure cases, reproducibility.

The hard parts, and the response

  • No pretrained detector: use scene-specific motion, beacon/livery cues and a from-scratch classifier; keep vision as a gate.
  • Few Indian event clips: curate 10–20 real events, add disclosed synthetic composites, and report both.
  • Lane-less traffic: reason about the approach arm in bird's-eye coordinates, not painted lanes.
  • Audio false alarms: hard negatives, K-of-N smoothing, and an operating point set by FP/hour.
  • Limited time: do not chase every cue. Ship the four-layer pipeline and a clean evaluation first.

What “from scratch” means here

  • No downloaded weights, including “backbone only” weights.
  • Classical models are fitted on our training split.
  • Any neural model is initialised randomly and trained by us.
  • Preprocessing (resize, mel filterbank) is allowed; it is not a pretrained model.
  • Every model card records data, hyperparameters, seed and commit.
The integration contract Each layer exposes a small, typed interface: audio → {p_siren, onset_s}; vision → tracks with cue scores; geometry → {arm, distance_m, eta_s}; fusion → {state, confidence, reason}; controller → signal phase plus log entry. This is what makes the system testable and the demo debuggable.

13 · Integrity

What we show, and what we do not claim

We will show

  • A working end-to-end software demo on a fixed junction.
  • Each layer's contribution through ablations.
  • A lead-time vs false-alarm curve with real and synthetic results separated.
  • Failure cases: night, occlusion, siren-like noise, wrong arm.
  • A reproducible benchmark and a one-command run.
  • A clear limitations and future-work section.

We will not claim

  • State-of-the-art detection accuracy.
  • Deployment on real traffic signals.
  • All-weather, all-camera robustness.
  • Direction of arrival from a single microphone.
  • That synthetic clips are real footage.
  • That the problem is “solved”.

Q1 Which novelty claim should we lead with?

Why we ask: the headline decides which experiment gets priority and how the report is framed. The candidates are the asymmetric fusion policy, arm-level lane-less preemption, and the SirenFirst-108 benchmark.

Q2 Is a benchmark of 10–20 real events acceptable?

Why we ask: real South Indian event clips are scarce. The answer decides whether synthetic composites can carry part of the evaluation, and whether the benchmark needs to be larger.

Q3 Which baselines must we compare against?

Why we ask: we cannot use pretrained YOLO inside the system, but an off-the-shelf detector could still be reported as an upper-bound reference. The answer decides whether that comparison is allowed or expected.

Q4 Is one fixed four-arm junction enough?

Why we ask: scope decides how much calibration and footage we need. One junction keeps the arm-level claim testable; more junctions strengthen generalisation but add significant work.

Q5 What robustness level is required?

Why we ask: night, rain and occlusion are expensive to cover. We need an explicit minimum so the evaluation scope is honest.

Q6 What should be the primary end-to-end metric?

Why we ask: we will report several numbers, but one has to be the headline in the abstract and viva. Median lead time at a fixed false-alarm rate is the current proposal.

Terms used on this page

SirenFirst-108The curated benchmark of South Indian 108 event clips, negatives and disclosed synthetic composites.
PreemptionTemporarily giving priority to one approach at a signal, while holding conflicting movements.
ArmOne of the four approaches to a junction: north, east, south or west. Not a painted lane.
Lead timeSeconds between the system's confirmed decision and the ambulance crossing the stop line.
False alarm per hourHow many times the system raises a confirmed alert in an hour of negative footage. Lower is better.
HomographyA one-time mapping from four points on the road to a flat bird's-eye view, so distances can be measured.
Bird's-eye viewA top-down rendering of the junction used to reason about arms and distance.
K-of-NA smoothing rule: require K detections out of the last N windows before raising an alert.
HysteresisUsing different thresholds to start and stop an alert, so it does not flicker.
FSMFinite-state machine: the explicit set of signal states and allowed transitions.
All-red clearanceA short period where all conflicting movements are red before another direction gets green.
HOG + SVMA classical image descriptor and classifier, trained from scratch on our crops.
Log-melA time-frequency representation of audio used as input to the siren model.
